## 1. EXECUTIVE PITCH & STORY ANGLE

### The Global Relevance
Corsica—a mountainous French island in the Mediterranean—is currently experiencing a silent, systemic transformation. Beneath its image as a pristine tourist paradise lies a complex financial and administrative scheme where **European and French public funds directly subsidize corporate land grabs, real estate speculation, and environmental degradation**.

This investigation moves beyond traditional narratives of local crime or regional nationalism. It exposes a **corporate-state-mafia ecosystem** where:
1.  **State Tax Incentives** (intended for local island development) are captured by national corporations and opaque holding companies via shell entities with zero employees.
2.  **European Subsidies** (PAC agricultural funds and ERDF post-COVID relief) are systematically siphoned through fraudulent declarations and administrative opacity.
3.  **Environmental Laws** (the *Loi Littoral* and regional planning codes) are bypassed through municipal zoning manipulations (PLUs) and executive decrees, threatening fragile ecosystems and water tables.

This dossier provides international investigative outlets (*The Guardian*, *Der Spiegel*, *El País*, *Le Monde*) with fully documented case studies, corporate registration numbers, cadastral coordinates, and geospatial verification data.

## 2. KEY INVESTIGATION CASE STUDIES

### CASE STUDY A: The Saint-Antoine Solar Power Scheme (Ajaccio)
*   **The Scheme:** Capturing renewable energy subsidies while risking environmental contamination on a public landfill.
*   **The Mechanism:** 
    *   The Municipality of Ajaccio awarded a 20-to-30-year lease on a 10.5-hectare former municipal landfill (CET Saint-Antoine) to the **SAS Centrale Photovoltaïque de Saint-Antoine**.
    *   Although backed by national energy giant **EDF Renewables**, the contract was awarded directly to a local project SAS registered with zero employees. This setup allowed the project to artificially qualify for the **Corsican Investment Tax Credit (CIIC - Art. 244 quater E CGI)**, claiming a 30% tax rebate funded by French taxpayers.
    *   **The Environmental Threat:** The site is a closed landfill sealed by a clay membrane to contain toxic leachate (heavy metal runoff). Driving thousands of steel piles into the ground without prior geotechnical soil studies risks breaching the membrane, threatening the Gravona river basin and local groundwater.
    *   **Legal Bypass:** To avoid public bidding mandatory for public domain concessions (CGPPP Art. L. 2122-1-1), the municipality claimed the site was private domain. When local courts struck down the zoning, an executive decree was issued to force the project through.

### CASE STUDY B: The "Corsica Invest" Real Estate Network (Ajaccio Coastline)
*   **The Scheme:** Speculative coastal development driven by influential local figures through overlapping corporate vehicles.
*   **The Mechanism:**
    *   Operated via **SAS CORSICA INVEST** (SIREN 888 027 182, registered in Ajaccio) and related construction entities (**PBDA Construction**, multiple SCIs).
    *   **The Infraction:** Massive non-compliant luxury villa developments along the Route des Sanguinaires (Ajaccio's high-value coastline). Constructions exceeded authorized surfaces, added illegal levels, and encroached directly onto protected natural areas and the Public Maritime Domain (DPM).
    *   **Administrative Complicity:** When courts ordered partial demolition and restoration of the site (*U Levante* litigation), municipal authorities issued alignment decrees on public roads (Boulevard Louis Campi / Rue des Magnolias) to retroactively legalize site encroachments. These decrees were subsequently suspended by the *Conseil d'État* for misuse of public authority.

### CASE STUDY C: The ERDF / Post-COVID Grant Fraud (May 2026 Raids)
*   **The Scheme:** Siphoning 18 Million Euros of European Recovery Funds.
*   **The Mechanism:**
    *   In May 2026, the **European Public Prosecutor’s Office (EPPO)** coordinated raids on the Corsican Economic Development Agency (**ADEC**), the Caisse de Développement de la Corse (**CADEC**), and regional offices.
    *   The investigation targets an estimated **€18M fraud** involving REACT-EU and ERDF funds. Subsidies intended for post-pandemic economic recovery were funneled into private corporate accounts through fictitious public procurement contracts and inflated invoices.
